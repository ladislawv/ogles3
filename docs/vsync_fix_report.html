ogles3: vsync swaps that wait two refreshes — investigation and fix

2026-11-16 · The report: a user's, "aglSwapBuffers with OGLES3_CCT_VSYNC sometimes waits two vblanks" (its VsyncTest.c is in tests/vsync/) · The work: branch vsync from gles3

Summary

With OGLES3_CCT_VSYNC on a screen of the library's own, aglSwapBuffers waited with WaitBOVP. On the test station WaitBOVP busy-polls the Pi's display scaler (HVS) until its output line equals the display's height, and the line has that value for only about 117 µs per refresh. Whenever the task lost the CPU across those 117 µs (another task, an interrupt), the poll missed the refresh and spun a whole refresh more: a swap of 33 ms, the frame shown twice. The spinning also took all the CPU below priority 10 for the length of every wait.

The swap now waits on the HVS's own frame counter. It sleeps on timer.device until shortly before the refresh, and if it wakes late it still sees that the refresh began, so the frame goes out at once instead of a refresh later.

How the cause was found

The card's source

Emu68-tools' VideoCore.card, cloned read-only in WSL ~/emu68-tools:

The reporter's program

VsyncTest (unchanged, tests/vsync/VsyncTest.c) reproduced the report: 14 swaps of 33 ms in 2940 here, 13 in the report.

M6's finding in display.c and DEVELOPMENT_PLAN §7.2, "WaitTOF and WaitBOVP follow the HDMI refresh and sleep", does not hold on this setup. The priority-10 boost had been measured with the earlier wait.

The fix

In ogles3/display.c and ogles3/ogles3_internal.h. On a screen of the library's own, and on one that a window covers completely:

PartWhat it does
hvs_probe
(at the first wait)		HVS channel 1 must be on (SCALER_DISPCTRL1 bit 31) with 200–4096 lines, and it must show two refreshes of 5–50 ms that agree within 300 µs. Otherwise the swap keeps WaitBOVP. The log names the method used.
hvs_wait: priorityThe task runs at priority 10 for the whole wait. It is asleep for most of it, so this costs nothing, and when woken it runs before every task below 10.
hvs_wait: sleepIt sleeps on timer.device until about 1 ms before the counter's change. The time left is worked out from the line, slightly short. Then it reads the counter until it changes.
hvs_wait: late wake-upA refresh that began after the last frame went out, while the scan is still at the top (in the picture's top eighth, or at the last line just before the counter moves), is taken at once: the task woke late. Further down the frame would tear, so it waits for the next refresh.
hvs_wait: stopped displayIf the counter doesn't move for 3 refreshes, the wait gives up instead of hanging.
Log at the context's destroyWaits, frames that went out at once, refreshes slept through (and how far into the frame those wake-ups came), and returns past the vertical blank.
